Scientific Context

The Disease: Hemophilia A & B

Hemophilia A

~1 in 5,000 male births — deficiency of Factor VIII. Severe disease is defined as FVIII activity <1%. Recurrent joint bleeds drive hemophilic arthropathy and long-term disability. The most common severe bleeding disorder.

Hemophilia B

~1 in 25,000 male births — deficiency of Factor IX. Moderately severe to severe disease defined as FIX ≤2%. Clinically similar to HA but historically underserved by subcutaneous prophylaxis options — a key gap this trial addresses.

Current Treatment Landscape

  • ◆
    Factor Replacement Therapy (IV) — Standard of care. Prophylactic or on-demand. Effective but requires IV access and frequent infusions; inhibitor development complicates management in ~30% of severe HA.
  • ◆
    Emicizumab (Hemlibra) — Bispecific antibody bridging FIXa and FX. SC, once-weekly to monthly. Transformative for HA — but approved for hemophilia A only, leaving HB patients without a comparable SC prophylaxis option.
  • ◆
    On-Demand Therapy — Treatment initiated only at time of bleeding. Prevalent in lower-resource settings. Associated with high ABRs, cumulative joint damage, and diminished quality of life.
  • ◆
    Gene Therapy — Emerging curative approach. HB has shown more durable responses due to smaller transgene size. Long-term durability and broad access remain active areas of study.

Adherence Burden

Frequent IV infusions for factor replacement create significant burden, particularly for pediatric patients and those with limited venous access.

HB Prophylaxis Gap

Prior to marstacimab, no SC prophylaxis option existed for hemophilia B — a critical therapeutic disparity relative to hemophilia A.

Breakthrough Bleeds

Even on established prophylaxis, patients experience breakthrough bleeds. Novel rebalancing mechanisms may offer superior or complementary protection.

Pharmacology

Mechanism of Action

Marstacimab
Anti-TFPI IgG1 Monoclonal Antibody • SC Once-Weekly
  • 1

    TFPI: The Natural Brake on Coagulation

    Tissue Factor Pathway Inhibitor (TFPI) is an endogenous anticoagulant that limits the extrinsic coagulation pathway by inhibiting Factor Xa and the TF/FVIIa initiating complex — normally a well-calibrated physiologic brake on thrombin generation.

  • 2

    Hemophilia: An Imbalanced Coagulation System

    When FVIII or FIX is absent or severely reduced, the intrinsic amplification of thrombin generation fails. TFPI’s continued inhibition of the extrinsic pathway leaves the system unable to generate sufficient thrombin for effective hemostasis — resulting in uncontrolled bleeding.

  • 3

    Marstacimab Releases the Brake

    By binding and neutralizing the Kunitz-2 domain of TFPI, marstacimab removes the endogenous inhibition of the extrinsic pathway — restoring thrombin generation independent of Factor VIII or IX. The mechanism is effective for both hemophilia A and B, and does not require co-administration of clotting factors.

  • 4

    A Non-Factor, Disease-Agnostic Approach

    Unlike emicizumab (HA-specific) or factor concentrates (deficiency-specific), marstacimab’s upstream TFPI-targeting mechanism is agnostic to the site of intrinsic pathway deficiency — making it a versatile rebalancing therapy applicable across hemophilia types.

Key Safety Context: A theoretical concern with anti-TFPI therapy is thrombosis — releasing the coagulation brake could, in principle, over-activate clotting. The complete absence of thromboembolic events across the full BASIS patient population is a clinically significant and reassuring safety signal that validates the therapeutic window of TFPI inhibition at the doses studied.
Key Takeaways

Clinical Significance

★ FDA Approved

First anti-TFPI monoclonal antibody approved for prophylaxis in hemophilia A and B without inhibitors — establishing a novel mechanism class in clinical practice.

🏛 Hemophilia B Breakthrough

First once-weekly subcutaneous prophylaxis option available for hemophilia B patients — addressing a longstanding and meaningful therapeutic gap relative to hemophilia A.

  • ✓
    91.6% ABR reduction vs. on-demand therapy demonstrates profound bleed prevention in a population previously relying on episodic treatment — a clinically and humanistically meaningful outcome.
  • ✓
    Noninferiority and superiority over prior factor prophylaxis in the RP group confirms that an SC option can achieve at least equivalent — and statistically superior — bleed control compared with IV prophylaxis.
  • ✓
    Fixed once-weekly SC dosing (not weight-based) simplifies administration, reduces clinical infrastructure requirements, and may meaningfully improve long-term adherence and patient autonomy.
  • ✓
    Zero thromboembolic events across the trial population directly refutes the primary safety concern of pro-coagulant therapy and validates the therapeutic window of TFPI inhibition.
  • ✓
    Disease-agnostic mechanism — anti-TFPI activity works for both HA and HB through a single drug, expanding the addressable patient population and simplifying the clinical decision framework.
